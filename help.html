1. Open a video

Click 📂 (Open) in the toolbar, File → Open video or audio… or drag a file onto the window. CaptionPress opens MP4, MOV, MKV, WEBM and audio files such as MP3, M4A and WAV. Your file is never uploaded – everything happens on this PC.

2. Create subtitles automatically

  1. Choose the Spoken language, or leave it on Detect automatically.
  2. Choose the Quality. Fast is good for clear English speech. Accurate, Very accurate and Most accurate (Pro) make far fewer mistakes with other languages, accents, music and noise. For Vietnamese and most other languages choose Most accurate (needs a graphics card) or Very accurate.
  3. Click ✨ Create subtitles. The lines appear on the right while CaptionPress listens.
The very first time, CaptionPress downloads the AI (80–750 MB depending on the quality). This happens only once. After that it works without internet.

On most PCs a 10-minute video takes 1–5 minutes. Click Stop at any time – the lines made so far are kept.

Have the lyrics or script already?

Open 📄 I have the lyrics or script above the Create button and paste the words – or click Open .txt file… (.lrc and .srt files work too). Put one subtitle per line. For songs, write out every repeated chorus. Then click ✨ Create subtitles: CaptionPress listens only to find when each line is sung or spoken, and keeps your words exactly as you typed them – no spelling mistakes. Lines like [Chorus] are skipped. Use Clear to go back to normal automatic subtitles.

3. Check and fix the lines

  • Click into any line and type to fix a word. The preview on the video updates at once.
  • ▶ plays that line. ✂ splits a line in two where your cursor is. ⤓ joins it with the next line. ✕ deletes it.
  • ＋ Line adds a new line at the current position of the video.
  • 🔎 finds and replaces a word everywhere – handy for names the AI spelled wrong.
  • A ⚠ warning shows lines that are too long or too fast to read comfortably.
  • ↶ Undo and ↷ Redo work for every change. Your lines are saved automatically and come back when you open the same video again.

4. Timing

  • Type a new start or end time, for example 1:23.4.
  • Or play the video and click ⇤ to start the line now, or ⇥ to end it now.
  • If all subtitles are a little early or late, click ⇄ Timing and move every line by a few tenths of a second.

5. Caption look

Open Caption look under the video. Click a ready-made style to start:

  • Classic – the whole line in a dark box, for any video.
  • TikTok bold – big capital letters with an outline; the word being spoken lights up.
  • Pop-up words – two or three big words at a time pop up in the middle, the way TikTok, Reels and Shorts creators do it.
  • Karaoke – the words fill with colour as they are spoken.
  • Box highlight – a dark box with the spoken word in colour.

Then change anything you like: Animation, Highlight colour, text size, position (bottom, middle or top), text colour, background, Font (Extra bold looks best on phones), CAPITAL LETTERS and letters per line (32 is good for phone videos, 42 is standard).

The word timing is worked out from each line's start and end, so it keeps working after you fix the words. If the highlight runs ahead or behind, split a long line into two shorter lines – each line gets its own exact time.

The preview shows exactly what the saved video will look like. Animations are kept when you save a video with captions burned in (Pro). SRT and VTT files keep only the words and times.

5a. Karaoke & lyrics videos

For songs, paste the lyrics in 📄 I have the lyrics or script (one sung line per line) and click ✨ Create subtitles. CaptionPress keeps your words and times every word to the singing. Then open Caption look and click Karaoke – 2 lines:

  • Lines take turns in an upper row (left) and a lower row (right). The next line already waits in the other row, so the singer can read ahead.
  • The sung part fills word by word in the Sung colour; the rest stays in the Unsung colour. Change both colours and the outlines as you like.
  • Signals (for Karaoke and Karaoke – 2 lines): before the first line and after an instrumental break, 4 signals count down – the last one goes away exactly when the singing starts. Choose the Signal shape (dots, music notes, triangles or squares) and how long a break must be (8 seconds or more by default).
  • Colour fill: Even moves the colour through every word at the speed it is sung – slowly over a long held note – and straight on through the tiny gaps between words; Smooth gives each word a soft start and end.
  • Karaoke timing: if the colour runs a little ahead of (or behind) the voice, move this slider – right = later, left = earlier. It changes only the highlight, not the line times.
  • Font: click the font button to use any font on your PC – Use fonts on this PC… lists them all (allow it when asked), or type a font name. Choose a font with Vietnamese letters for Vietnamese songs.
  • Move left / right, Move up / down – or simply drag the caption on the video – to place the lyrics anywhere. Line on screen puts a line on the left, in the centre or on the right. Every slider shows its value – positions go from −1 (far left / down) through 0 (the default place) to +1 (far right / up) – and ↺ Reset to default puts size and position back.
  • With Karaoke – 2 lines the Upper line and Lower line each have their own Line on screen (left, centre or right – by default the upper line on the left and the lower line on the right), Left / right, Up / down, Size (50–200 %) and Width (narrower or wider letters, 50–200 %). The saved video looks exactly like the preview, and the settings are kept in the project file.
Free users can try Karaoke – 2 lines in the preview (it shows a “PREVIEW ★ CaptionPress Pro” mark). Saving the karaoke video – File → Export → 🎬 Video with captions – is part of Pro.

Save your work as a project

File → 💾 Save project (or Ctrl+S) saves a .cpproj file with all lines and word times, the caption look, the lyrics and the name of the video. File → 📂 Open project… – or a double-click on the .cpproj file in Windows (with the CaptionPress app installed from the Microsoft Store) – brings it all back – then open the same video again. CaptionPress warns you if the video looks different from the one the project was made for.

When the colour does not follow the singing

The karaoke colour moves word by word inside each line. With Options → Karaoke highlight follows my time changes switched on (it is on at the start), the words are fitted to a line again every time you change its start or end. To fix lines that were changed before, use Edit → 🎤 Re-make the karaoke highlight…: choose all lines or only the selected line, and Fit to each line’s time (keeps the rhythm of the singing) or Spread the words evenly. Ctrl+Z goes back.

5b. Exact timing with Tap timing

Automatic timing of sung music is never perfect. For exact karaoke, time the lines yourself – it takes about as long as the song:

  1. Paste the lyrics (or make the subtitle lines first), then click 🎵 Tap timing (above the subtitle lines or under the lyrics).
  2. Choose How to tap: Hold Space while each line is sung (press when the line starts, let go when it ends), Tap once when each line starts, or Tap every word for an exact word-by-word highlight. Choose a Speed – 0.5× or 0.75× makes it easier.
  3. Click ▶ Start. The line to sing is shown big, the next one under it. Use Space or the big button.
  4. Made a mistake? Backspace or ← goes back one line and plays it again. Esc or ✓ Finish stops – the lines you timed are kept; open Tap timing again to go on from the next line.

The colour of a line's first word starts exactly when the voice starts (CaptionPress puts it on the sung onset near your key press); the line itself appears a moment before. The words inside each line are spread by syllable length, so the karaoke colour still fills word by word. Ctrl+Z undoes a whole tapping session. Tap timing is free.

Fine-tune one line

Click a line in the list: a bar appears with Start and End buttons (−0.1, −0.05, +0.05, +0.1 seconds). Keys: [ / ] move the start earlier / later, Shift+[ / Shift+] the end, add Alt for 0.1 s. Tick Move the lines after it too to shift everything that follows. After each change the line plays once so you hear the result.

5c. Effects, title card and video shape

Open ✨ Effects, title & video shape under the video:

  • Line effect: each line fades, zooms or slides in, and fades out when it is done.
  • Glow around the sung words, Outline thickness (0–300 %), and a Two-colour fill (the sung colour runs into a second colour from top to bottom).
  • Duet: click a line in the list and choose Who sings in the bar above the lines – Singer A, Singer B or Both. Each has its own sung colour.
  • Title card: song title, singer and “Music & lyrics” shown at the start for 3, 5 or 8 seconds.
  • Video shape: the same as the video, or 1080p 16:9 (YouTube), 1080×1920 9:16 (TikTok, Reels, Shorts) or 1080×1080 1:1. The video is fitted inside; the space around it shows the Background colour or a Background picture (darkened as you choose). For an MP3 or other sound file the picture fills the whole video – a lyrics video from a song and one picture.
The preview shows exactly what the saved video looks like. These effects, Karaoke – 2 lines and Signals are part of Pro in the saved video; without Pro you can try them in the preview, which then shows a “PREVIEW ★ CaptionPress Pro” mark. The background picture is not kept in the project file – choose it again after restarting.

5b. Beat karaoke, interlude screen, logo and second language (Pro)

  • 🎤 Beat karaoke (Options → Beat karaoke, or the panel under the video): Singer’s voice at 0 % takes the vocals out with AI so only the music is left – a karaoke beat. 10–20 % keeps a soft guide voice. Key makes the song higher or lower (in semitones) for your voice, Speed makes it slower or faster; the lyrics follow. Click 🎵 Make the beat, tick Hear the beat with the video and press play. The vocal remover downloads once (about 170 MB) and then works offline; with a graphics card a 5-minute song takes about a minute and a half. Save it with 💾 Save the beat (.wav), or tick Use the beat when you save the video with captions. Free: try it on the first 30 seconds.
  • 🎼 Interlude screen (Effects): in long music breaks the text you choose (for example ♪ Nhạc dạo ♪) and a bar that fills up until the singing starts again.
  • 🏷 Your logo (Effects): choose a picture (PNG with a see-through background looks best), the corner, the size and the strength. It is remembered on this computer.
  • 🌐 Second language line (Effects): paste the translation – one line for each lyrics line – and click Use these lines. Each line then shows its translation in smaller letters underneath. A repeated chorus needs its translation only once. The translations are kept in the project file.

6. Save your subtitles

Click 📤 Export in the toolbar, or File → Export:

  • .srt – works with YouTube, Facebook, LinkedIn, Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, CapCut, VLC and most players.
  • .vtt – for websites and some video platforms.
  • Transcript – the text without or with times, for notes, blog posts or show notes.

The file goes to your Downloads folder. To edit an existing subtitle file, choose File → Import → Subtitle file.

Recent projects and templates

File → 🕘 Recent projects lists the last 10 projects you opened or saved – click one to open it again (then open its video). File → 🎨 Templates keeps your favourite caption looks: set up the look and effects, choose Save as template… and give it a name; it appears under My templates for every new video. Default template brings back the original look. Save template file… / Load template file… move a template to another PC as a .cptemplate file.

7. Video with captions burned in (Pro)

For TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts the words should be part of the picture. Choose File → Export → 🎬 Video with captions and click Start. CaptionPress plays the video once and records it with your captions, so it takes as long as the video. Keep the window open until it is finished.

7a. Translate the subtitles (Pro)

CaptionPress can turn English subtitle lines into Vietnamese, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Chinese, Japanese, Russian, Ukrainian, Indonesian, Hindi, Arabic, Dutch, Swedish, Danish, Finnish, Czech, Romanian or Hungarian.

  1. Make English subtitle lines. If the video is in another language, choose Subtitles in: English (translate) before you click Create subtitles.
  2. Open 🌐 Translate the subtitles, choose the language and click Translate lines.
  3. Check the translated lines. Ctrl + Z brings the English back.
  4. The AI voice is switched to the new language, so you can go straight to 🎙 AI voice-over.
Each language downloads once (about 100 MB). Machine translation is good but not perfect – read the lines once before you publish.

7b. AI voice-over (Pro)

An AI voice can read your subtitle lines aloud, each at the moment it appears – great for translated videos, tutorials and faceless videos.

  1. Open 🎙 AI voice-over under the video and pick a Voice. There are natural voices for more than 20 languages, including English, Vietnamese, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Russian and Chinese.
  2. Click ▶ Try this voice to hear it (free).
  3. Click 🎙 Create voice-over. Tick Hear it with the video and press play.
  4. Use Original sound to keep the original audio quietly in the background (for example 20%) or turn it off.
  5. Save with File → Export → Video with AI voice-over, or save only the voice as a WAV file.
Each voice is downloaded once (about 60 MB) and then works offline. If a line is too long for its time, CaptionPress speaks it a little faster so it ends before the next line.

7e. Remove “um” and cut long pauses

Filler words in the subtitles (free)

Click ✂ Ums above the subtitle lines. CaptionPress removes “um”, “uh”, “er”, “hmm” and similar words (also “ờ”, “ừm”, “euh”, “äh”) from every line and tidies the commas. Press Ctrl+Z to undo.

A shorter video without long pauses (Pro)

  1. Open Edit → ✂ Cut out long pauses… (or File → Export → ✂ Shorter video).
  2. Choose how long a pause must be before it is cut: 0.5 seconds for a fast, punchy video, 0.8 seconds for a natural one.
  3. CaptionPress shows how many pauses it found and how much shorter the video will be. Click Save shorter video. It plays the video once with the pauses skipped, so it takes about as long as the new video.
  4. Want subtitles for the new video? Click Move subtitle times to match, then save the SRT file.
A little quiet is always kept before and after each sentence, so words are never cut off.

8. Keyboard shortcuts

  • Space or Ctrl + Space – play / pause
  • ← → – back / forward 2 seconds
  • Ctrl + Z / Ctrl + Y – undo / redo
  • Ctrl + H – find and replace
  • Ctrl + O – open a video
  • Ctrl + S – save the project
  • [ ] – move the start of the selected line (with Shift: the end; with Alt: 0.1 s)
  • In Tap timing: hold Space while a line is sung, Backspace back one line, Esc stop

9. Free version and Pro

  • Free: Fast quality, subtitles for the first 10 minutes of each video, full editing, SRT, VTT and transcript files.
  • Pro (one-time purchase): videos of any length, Accurate, Very accurate and Most accurate quality, translation into English, translating subtitles into 19 languages, videos with captions burned in, and AI voice-over.
  • 7-day Pro trial: click ★ Get Pro → 🎁 Try Pro free for 7 days to use every Pro feature for a week. During the trial nothing can be saved or exported (projects can still be saved) – buy Pro to save your work, or click End the trial to go back to the free version. The trial can be used once.

10. If something goes wrong

  • “Could not read the sound”: the file may have no sound track or an unusual format. Try converting it to MP4 first.
  • It is slow and says “slower mode”: your PC has no graphics acceleration available for the AI. Use Fast quality, or update your graphics driver.
  • The download of the AI stopped: check your internet connection and click Create subtitles again. It continues where it stopped.
  • Wrong language: choose the spoken language instead of Detect automatically.
  • Many wrong words in Vietnamese or another language: choose the spoken language and a more accurate quality. Fast is made for English.
  • “Most accurate” needs a graphics card: if your PC has no graphics acceleration, choose Very accurate.
